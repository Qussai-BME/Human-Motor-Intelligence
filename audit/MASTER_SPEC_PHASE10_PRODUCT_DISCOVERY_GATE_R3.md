# Master Specification Phase 10 — Product-Discovery Gate
## V2 Recovery R3 Current-State Deferral Record

## Decision

> **Product discovery is intentionally DEFERRED.** The programme now provides a reviewable local-first Research-OS surface, but it has no evidence of repeated external researcher use, validated pain points, willingness to pay, or permission to process external data. Therefore it is not a commercial product, SaaS, medical device, clinical system, or hosted benchmark service.

## Researcher-Readiness Assets

| Available asset | Plausible research-user value hypothesis | Current boundary |
|---|---|---|
| Metadata-first registry with protocol/run/artifact identity | Traceable experiment bookkeeping and review | Local single-writer MVP; not team/multi-tenant infrastructure |
| `hmi doctor`, integrity, typed navigation, and manifest verification | Faster reviewer/researcher inspection of evidence boundaries | No execution-as-a-service or hidden data upload |
| Subject-level benchmark/report engine | Reusable evidence-safe reporting patterns | No global leaderboard or automatic model-selection claim |
| Public-review route and Word/manuscript outputs | Faster technical review and paper support | Not a substitute for peer review or clinical validation |
| P3/P6 gate controls | Prevent unsupported expansions and hidden scientific debt | Gates require owner-supplied evidence, not product workflows |

## Explicitly Absent by Design

The release contains no billing, subscription, telemetry, user accounts, cloud runtime, multi-tenancy, remote data upload, background job service, or commercial traction claim. No workflow transmits biosignal data outside the researcher’s controlled local environment.

## Minimum Future Discovery Protocol

A future pilot may begin only with consented external researchers and an approved local-first prototype. It should test whether protocol registration, artifact provenance, manifest verification, and review routing solve a recurring workflow problem. The pilot must record user role, task, time burden, observed failure points, repeated use, and consented qualitative feedback; it must not collect raw biosignals or covert telemetry.

A commercialization decision requires repeated demonstrated value across more than one external research context, a clear governance model, data-handling review, and explicit willingness-to-pay evidence. Until then, all commercial language remains hypothetical.[1] [2]
