# Commercialization Notes
## Evidence-Bound Product Discovery Position

## Current Position

Human Motor Intelligence is currently a local research integration and evidence-governance stack. It is **not** a medical device, clinical decision-support product, prosthetic controller, commercial SaaS, hosted data platform, or certified safety system. The retained research outputs are insufficient to support product-performance, safety, reimbursement, or clinical-effectiveness claims.

## Presently Reusable Product-Learning Assets

| Asset | Legitimate product-discovery value | Non-permitted interpretation |
|---|---|---|
| Versioned contracts and manifests | Traceability, auditability, and research operations design | Interoperability certification or regulated-device compliance |
| Local registry and reproducibility tooling | Internal research workflow and evidence lineage | Multi-tenant product/security service |
| P4/P5 source-bound results | Hypothesis formation and benchmark design | Universal decoder performance or clinical usability |
| P6 hard-stop workflow | Provenance controls that prevent false systems claims | Embodied-control efficacy or device safety |
| Q1 manuscript/review apparatus | Reviewer-resilient documentation practice | Publication acceptance or regulatory approval |

## Preconditions for Any Product Discovery Expansion

A separate product-discovery effort would require a defined user/problem statement, security/privacy review, data-governance framework, safety and risk analysis, clinical/regulatory assessment where applicable, validated deployment environment, usability evaluation, and explicit governance of model updates. None of these requirements may be inferred from an offline external validation or simulator fixture.

## Responsible Narrative

The appropriate current narrative is that the programme provides **research leverage**: it connects biosignal experiments, provenance, reproducibility, and controlled systems testing while exposing evidence gaps before they become overstated claims. Commercial opportunity, if evaluated later, should be treated as a new research-and-governance programme with its own specifications, rather than as an implication of the current artefacts.
